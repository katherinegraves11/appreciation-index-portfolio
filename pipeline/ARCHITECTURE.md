# The Appreciation Index: how it works, every day

This is the single plan the tool, the scans and every agent follow. It collects Katie's directives to 2026-10-24. When a directive changes, this file changes first. The detailed scan schedule is in SCAN_MAP.md; the daily task follows both.

## 1. Who it is for

1. **The boutique buyer.** They bought, or can buy, a bag new in store. Their question: what is it worth secondhand today, and what would I make if I sold it right away? Or should I hold it?
2. **The secondhand buyer.** They are buying a pristine or worn bag from a website or a private sourcer. Their question: is this price right for this bag in this condition, and what will it be worth later?

Every answer serves both, kept apart, and never tells either one "never resell".

## 2. Rules that are never broken

1. **No hallucination, no bias.** Every number traces to a row with its source and date. Say what a figure rests on. Present both sides.
2. **Photo store (Katie, 2026-10-25).** Photos are kept privately in the backend for cross-referencing: customer photos when the customer opts in ("Keep my photos"), Katie's photos, and listing photos (with their source link) from the sites we read. They are never shown on the front end or redistributed, and can be deleted on request. Known risk, accepted by Katie: sites' terms often forbid copying their images.
2. **Cite, don't copy.** Store our own words and numbers only: no images, captions, handles or copied text. Comments are counted as themes; quotes are five words or fewer.
3. **Never sign in, never get past a login, CAPTCHA, paywall or bot check.** Read at human pace, one page about every 4 seconds, in Katie's Chrome where a site needs it.
4. **Private stays private.** Dior decks and preview items are never shown. Katie's sourcer quotes may be shown (her decision).
5. **Nothing customers see changes without Katie's yes.** Changes go through the customer agents and Margot first; experiments go through Theo.
6. **Sales data is never deleted.** A listing that disappears is kept as a likely sale at its last price.
7. **Never a gap without a solution (non-negotiable).** A gap in our records is not a gap in the market. Ask "where is that information available?" (The RealReal, Madison Avenue Couture, Fashionphile, Sotheby's, Christie's, eBay sold, the house's sites, the price guides, the sourcer), fetch it, store it with its source, then answer. Say "thin" only after saying what was checked.
8. **Katie's purchases are real sales, always used (bottom line, Katie, 2026-10-25: "This is only as good as proprietary information").** Every bag Katie buys, and every price her sourcer shares, is logged the same day and counts as a completed sale in the price range for every shopper who asks about that bag, including when she asks about it herself. Customer pages call it "a private purchase by a luxury shopper through a sourcer" and never name her or the sourcer.

## 3. What we collect

### A. Boutique prices: the only absolute price

1. **House catalogues, 16 houses.** Every US handbag on each house's site: name, reference, price, material, colour, hardware, availability.
   - Read every page and every collection page linked from it (for example Chanel's embossed-leather and Coco Neige pages).
   - Weekly, and on the day of any drop.
2. **Hermès.** Hermès blocks our Chrome. Use Katie's screenshots, plus the price guides (PurseBop, Sotheby's) for Birkin and Kelly, which Hermès doesn't publish.
3. **Price history.** Every boutique price keeps its date, so price rises are measured.

### B. What bags really sell for: willingness to pay

1. **The RealReal: daily, through Katie's Chrome, no exceptions (Katie, 2026-10-24: "Do not ever make an excuse about the RealReal").** Sold-first model searches for every covered house, each house's newest handbags, every special finish, trending and on-hold listings. If the scheduled run cannot use Chrome, any session that can runs it the same day; "skipped" is never an acceptable status.
   The detail: Sold and for sale, condition and year. Product pages are opened for high-value, exotic, special-order and newly sold listings, recording year, hardware, size, leather and inclusions.
2. **Madison Avenue Couture:** weekly, Monday. Full store; new, pre-owned and vintage kept apart. Listings that vanish are checked on their own page, then kept as "gone at last ask".
3. **Fashionphile, Rebag, FWRD Renew, Privé Porter, Jane Finds:** asks and sold or placed pages, weekly.
4. **Auctions:** results as they post.
5. **Special-order pickups:** daily. Ask TRR "custom-made Hermès bags from this year", plus the sourcer sites' special-order pages.
6. **Katie's sourcer quotes and purchases:** logged as data points. A daily agent (9:45 AM) reads new texts from her luxury sourcer in Messages on this Mac. It looks at each bag photo once to name the bag, and records each price as boutique-via-sourcer or a sourcer price, plus anything Katie bought. Derived data only: no message text, no images, no names on customer-facing pages, and never a message sent.

### C. The comparison, on every listing

Each resale listing is matched to its boutique price, daily. Model, size, type and leather must agree. Vintage is kept separate. A listing gets no match rather than a wrong one.

### D. Culture: what will move prices next

1. **News:** Vogue Business, WWD, Business of Fashion, Puck, WGSN.
2. **Search interest:** weekly Google search history.
3. **Social listening:** Instagram and TikTok, read in Katie's Chrome by the scan itself. Then Lena does the web, search and market half.
   - Every moment saved has a plain one-sentence version for customers (public_line). If it's about one leather (the Kylie Jenner suede Birkin), it also names that leather, so it only shows on answers about that leather.
4. **Faces of the house:** each house's ambassadors and most-photographed wearers, from cited sources. Every carry of a covered bag is logged; a carry before the bag is in stores is flagged "pre-release".
5. **Runway and re-see:** every show captured from Vogue Runway, plus the re-see and "first look at the bags" posts. Each bag is recorded with its in-store window. Comments are counted to see which bags people single out.
6. **Katie's calls:** her picks are saved as her judgment, then tested against asks, saves and sales once the bags land.

## 4. The daily waterfall

1. **Read:** retail, resale, news, WGSN.
2. **Match:** every listing to its boutique price.
3. **Listen:** every moment the reads surfaced goes through social listening. These are celebrity carries, designer moves, drops, viral posts and price rises.
4. **Connect:** line up willingness to pay and sales against culture for every tracked bag (the connective tissue rule).
5. **Forecast:** a forward read for each bag, labelled as a forecast with its confidence.
6. **Digest:** what changed, what sold, what needs Katie.

**Triggers on top of the daily run:**
- **A drop:** read that house the same day.
- **Fashion weeks** (both Paris seasons, haute couture, cruise, Métiers d'Art): runway and re-see capture.
- **Earnings and people moves:** the house's results page and an ownership check.

## 5. Keeping it complete and honest

1. **Coverage check.** It runs four times a day, inside the watchdog. For each house: bags held against the site's own total, and how recently we read it. A gap means a full re-read next run. No house is "complete" until the check says so.
2. **Matching errs strict.** No match is better than a wrong one.
3. **Two sources** before a moment is called VERIFIED; otherwise it's UNCONFIRMED.
4. **Ruth (operations)** checks every job ran. **Sam (engineering)** checks numbers trace to rows.
5. **The 5pm panel** reads the live tool every day.

## 6. How an answer is built

1. **Read the question,** keeping the client's own words ("I just bought this in store", a quoted price, a condition).
2. **Pull the numbers,** in about 35 seconds:
   - the boutique price for that size and leather;
   - sold and asking prices for the same model, size and material;
   - sourcer quotes;
   - the forward read;
   - what the house showed for next season.
3. **Guardrail:** check that the tracker holds prices for the exact version asked about, its finish as well as its model (Himalaya, diamond hardware, crocodile, ostrich, fringe, suede...). If it doesn't, search the web for that version while the answer runs (it takes longer). Save what that search finds, so the next question about that version is quick. Plain-version prices never stand in for a special version.
4. **Write the answer** to the editorial rules.
5. **Reuse the same day.** An answer for the same bag is reused only on the same day (New York time), with that day's numbers. A question with a quoted price or a condition always runs fresh. Prices move, so answers are never reused across days.
   - **Reopened reads (Katie, 2026-11-06):** a saved read opened again (shared link, /brief, My bags, partner API) is re-written by code from that day's numbers, on show only. The saved brief is never changed. Reads from before the current answer shape show as saved. HELD 2026-11-07 after the panel split 3 to 3 (branch reopened-reads-held): fix the shared code bugs first, then a dated version (saved words kept, today's update shown with its date) goes back to the panel.
   - **The forever card (Katie, 2026-11-10, after four panel rounds; findings/panel/2026-11-07-forever-card-fixes.md):** a shape or finish is dated only from tracker rows that name their source, with the source shown (Sotheby's first, PurseBop where nothing else covers it), the reader's bag first. A rare finish never borrows the plain leather's boutique prices; where none is held it says "We have no boutique price for this finish; we judge it on resale and auction prices." A boutique path names its leather and size when the bag asked differs. Auction results count only when the auction's own model is the bag asked about. Open, Katie's calls: the So Kelly verdict (discontinued 2016, still "Forever bag"), counting non-dollar auction results, explaining "Himalaya".

4. **Social listening on every bag asked (Katie, 2026-10-24: "This is not just a numbers situation. This is cultural knowledge").** Each new bag and finish gets a press and search read (Google, Vogue, Vogue Business, Harper's Bazaar, WWD, PurseBop, PurseBlog, Sotheby's, Christie's), kept for 7 days:
   - its standing: how rare and coveted it is, who can get one, nicknames, rising or fading;
   - its dated cultural moments (celebrity carries, records, viral posts), saved to the moments table, VERIFIED with two sources, else UNCONFIRMED.
   The answer weighs standing alongside the numbers. Moments shown are about that exact bag and finish: a Himalaya answer shows Himalaya moments, never the Kylie bag wall.
5. **Collector bags** (Himalaya, porosus, diamond hardware). The boutique buyer gets two choices with figures: sell now for the gain, or keep it as a collector's piece for ten years or more, citing the auction history by year. Auction history is collected for these finishes from cited sources.

### The answer always gives

1. **For a boutique buyer: what they would keep selling it the same day,** after a typical resale fee, in dollars. If that's 50% or more above what they paid, say "consider selling it right away". If the boutique price is missing, ask what they paid.
   - **Best time to sell:** name one moment (now, in about a year, or in about five years) with the figure for each. Choose now when the premium is 50% or more and trends aren't rising. A known premium today beats a forecast.
2. **For a secondhand buyer: is this price right for this condition?** Judge it against what that condition sells for, both ways: a worn bag priced well can be a good buy. A price from a listing on any resale site counts as their quote. Then say what they'd get back reselling it (right away, in a year, in five years) as a share of what they paid, after fees.
   - **Time value of money (Katie, 2026-10-24):** money now beats money later, and heritage shocks or revived popularity won't be known for years. The 1-, 5- and 10-year cards lead with dollar figures; future figures are also shown in today's money (4% a year). A gain after fees today is said first. A moment's demand fades, so a moment-made gain is best taken while it lasts.
   - **Secondhand buyers can flip too:** when their price sits below what the same version actually sells for (sales, not asks, at least three), say so with the figure.
   - **Cards:** Yes = a gain after fees on 5+ same-version sales, or 3+ with a rising forward read. "Watching" says exactly what turns it to Yes. No = a loss after fees on real sales.
3. **Three prices:** the boutique price (the only absolute one); website sales and asks (averages, with counts); and private sourcer prices.
4. **Coming next:** what the house has shown for next season and when it lands. Call a coming bag a possible hit only when the evidence singles it out, and call that an early read.
5. **Plain words:** sales are "sold", asks are "asks". Never "no sales"; say "secondhand sourcer", never "dealer".
6. **What it rests on:** a counted line naming its sources, for example "Based on the 5 sales and 3 asking prices we found for this bag (suede, size 30) on The RealReal and Jane Finds · a fair read". Every count is what we found in the sites we track, never a census: never "only N exist" or "there are no sales" (Katie, 2026-10-24). Hermès suede is listed as Doblis or Grizzly; search both. There is no jargon stamp like "Core Hold" or "confidence medium".

## Signal key (Katie, 2026-10-25)
One fixed table in code (bag-index/src/lib/signalKey.ts) says what a page signal means; every answer reads signals through it.
- Out of stock at the boutique = demand (supports holding value; never a price on its own).
- On hold on The RealReal = a sale, counted with the sales at its listed price, labelled "on hold".
Rows are added only on Katie's word. The daily scan records stock status on every house page read and keeps the On Hold tag on every RealReal tile.

## House-site pieces are the current designer's (Katie, 2026-10-25; never-change rule 5)
A link to, or screenshot from, a house's own site is a new bag at the boutique from the house's current designer, never vintage. Links are read by the product reference in the address against our catalogue reads (chanel.com AS6482B2605694305 → Maxi Flap Bag, $8,800); a house link we have not read asks for the price, and a typed price with it is the boutique price, never a pre-loved quote.

## The ticker is market news, never our workings (Katie, 2026-10-25)
"I really do like the rotating text at the top, but 'What moved today?' is giving away too much information about what the tool is actually doing."
- **In:** the houses and hot bags; what social listening heard; the economy (house results, demand by region); the best sales clocked (top auction, top sale this week, a bag that sold years after its stamp); sold out at the boutique (signal key); Paris Fashion Week and other shows; designer moves.
- **Never:** counts of what we collect (listings read, asks, shelves), source names of our reads, "since the last read", or anything that shows how the tool works.

Every note line is a fact we opened and stored; unverified claims never go in. The daily scan adds notes from verified signals.

## The snapshot: three numbers a customer can trust (Katie, 2026-10-26)
"All a lot of redundant information. I don't know what to trust as a customer. How do we create one incredibly accurate snapshot? … one range of recent sales second hand including luxury sourcer numbers, one range of currently available online second hand sales including luxury sourcer numbers, and confirmed sales directly from boutique. I think that's all we need from a customer perspective."
- **Sold secondhand:** one range of real recent sales of this exact version: The RealReal sold and on-hold (signal key), sourcer placements (Madison Avenue Couture, Privé Porter, Jane Finds…), private purchases, auctions in the last five years. With four or more, the typical range (middle half); fewer, the full spread. The headline uses this same range, so the page never shows two different "sells for" numbers.
- **For sale now:** one range of current secondhand asking prices for the same exact version, online and at sourcers, typical range the same way. Never mixed into the sold range on the page.
- **New at the boutique:** the house's current price for this exact version (house site or confirmed boutique purchase), with its size and leather.
- Each shows its count and sources in one short line. Nothing else repeats these numbers: no separate receipts strip, no "Based on…" line, no prices line inside "show me why".

## The customer journey (Katie, 2026-10-26)
Environment: a tech adopter (millennial, Gen Z). The whole journey must feel easy.
1. **Spots a bag of interest.** Not sure about the price, or whether it is the right addition to a growing luxury collection.
2. **Drops a photo or asks a question** (photo drop box, a pasted listing link, or typed words).
3. **Gets affirming or cautionary information**: informative points for and against, first as a solo buy, and later, once the tool has amassed the person's collection (My bags), compared with their full collection.

How today's page serves it: the Yes/No box (now, by how they're buying), the four boxes (resell in a year, hold three years, forever, just to wear), the snapshot (sold secondhand, for sale now, new at the boutique), then "show me why" folded (six cards, one cheeky line each).
Later phases, each designed whole before any code: (a) the collection view, which compares a bag with what the person already owns once My bags holds several; (b) a live agent chat box for follow-up questions on an answer.

## Storage & knowledge (Katie, 2026-10-31)
"Create a RAG and move all the storage for this tool to a cloud … as we collect more data and store it the more full my computer will be." Then: "write architecture, add a storage & knowledge card, and move forward with all this." The tool was unfrozen the same day.

**Measured 2026-10-31.** All of Fashion Data Lab is 190 MB (luxury_bags.db 37 MB: 15,857 RealReal reads, 27,719 listings; raw 130 MB; photos 8.5 MB). The Mac is 93% full (33 GB free of 460 GB), so the space is being used by something else. The real reasons to move are that jobs stop when the laptop sleeps or waits for a permission (Oct 27–29 collected nothing), there are three copies (laptop, Render, Dropbox), and photos need a proper private home.

**1. One master, kept in the cloud (Cloudflare R2, private bucket).**
- The databases stay SQLite. The app (node:sqlite) and every script already speak it, so nothing is rewritten.
- Litestream streams every change in luxury_bags.db and trends.db to R2 within seconds; any copy can be rebuilt to any minute. The nightly dated backups and the Dropbox copy carry on as before (never deleted).
- Photos, raw reads and backups are synced to the same private bucket. Once a file is confirmed in R2, it may leave the laptop; nothing is deleted from R2.
- Cost (Cloudflare's price page, 2026-10-31): the first 10 GB a month are free, then $0.015 per GB a month; no download fees. At today's size this is $0.
- Katie only: create the Cloudflare account, the bucket and the access key, and paste the key into the laptop's keychain herself.

**2. The knowledge layer (the RAG), for words, never for prices.**
- Prices, sales and ranges always come from exact SQL on the tracker, as now. Retrieval never produces a number.
- `db/knowledge.db` holds every written thing we know, chunked, with its source, date and status: the news reads (findings/news), moments, people moves, PESTEL and supply-chain facts, history, shows, stakeholder decisions, web findings, WGSN signals and reports, and panel decisions.
- Search is SQLite full-text search (BM25) now, at no cost; meaning-based search (Voyage embeddings, 200 million tokens free; then sqlite-vec) is added once keyword search misses things in testing.
- Every chunk carries a **visibility**:
  - `public`: our own words with a cited source (news reads, people moves, facts tables, moments' public lines);
  - `licensed`: WGSN and Muse, which inform the read but are never quoted or named to customers;
  - `private`: the panel, sourcer judgments, decks and daily digests, which are never shown.
- Status travels with each chunk: VERIFIED, UNCONFIRMED or single source. An answer may cite a public chunk with its date and status; it may not upgrade an UNCONFIRMED one.
- Built by `scripts/build_knowledge.py` (rebuildable from its sources at any time) and queried with `scripts/ask_knowledge.py`.
- **Into the answer page (needs the panel, then Katie's yes):** a "what's happening" card in "show me why" for the exact house and bag, from public chunks only. Then the follow-up chat box (roadmap: Later) reads the same layer.

**3. Jobs off the laptop, in order.**

## The golden set (Katie, 2026-10-31)
"Lets do this." Real customer questions from the question library, each with the bag reading a person verified, kept in `golden_questions` (luxury_bags.db). Drafts until Katie approves them.
- **Each row holds:** the question; the verified house, bag and spec; must-words and must-not words for checking a reading; how they're buying (new / used) and any quoted price; anything Katie must decide (`needs_katie`); and a dated baseline sold range. The baseline is a check, never an answer.
- **The test** (`bag-index/scripts/golden.mts`, add `--reading` for the AI check) runs after every change to how questions are read or priced:
  - READING: the AI names the verified bag;
  - FAST: an approved question finds its own reading with no AI call;
  - NUMBERS: today's range against the baseline, with DRIFT flagged past 35% for a person to look at.
  Results go to findings/golden/.
- **Faster answers (Katie: "YES. Faster").** `goldenReading()` in questions.ts lets an approved golden reading replace the AI read of a new question.
  - It applies only when the question names the same must-words and exactly the same detail words: size, leather, colour, hardware, condition, year and shape.
  - Condition counts only when said as a condition ("fair condition"; "is $9,500 fair?" is about the price).
  - It never applies to a comparison, a second house, or two golden readings that both fit. Anything else goes to the AI read as before.
  - Prices and verdicts are never cached: they are rebuilt from the tracker on every answer.
- **First draft (2026-10-31):** 65 questions across 11 houses. Baseline ranges for 60. Five have no sold range in our data (croc-embossed Small Shopping Bag, Oversized CC Flap preorder, Coco Beach Clownfish Clutch, Dior Saddle with Strap in denim strass and in newspaper print): never-a-gap work.
